# MC / Symbol studies

Built-in image generation concepts for matthewcoward.com. Preference: simple, understated and refined; the initials form part of a recognizable object, with M and C still identifiable.

1. **Folded page:** an open-book result; the C is readable, but the M needs work.
2. **Sail / ears:** the sail brief produced a more cat-like outline. The angular M and lower C are the clearest pair in this round.
3. **Bird:** a readable bird and C, with a less explicit M in the wings.

## Exact prompts

### 01-folded-page

Use case: logo-brand. Original logo for Matthew Coward / matthewcoward.com. The design brief is a simple understated and refined object-symbol constructed from the initials M and C, with BOTH initials identifiable. A visual double reading: the actual strokes of the letters constitute the object. The client liked an example where a few rounded monogram strokes form a tulip, but DOES NOT WANT A FLOWER. Do not copy a tulip. Use clean medium-weight rounded monoline black strokes on pure white; no fills except small necessary details. Restrained, elegant, few elements, optically balanced, lots of breathing room. One logo centered large on white, no labels, no wordmark, no captions or explanatory diagrams. No mockups, no shading, gradients, texture or 3D. Do not draw an object and then print MC inside or beside it. Keep the letter shapes recognizable. Concept: FOLDED PAGE / OPEN BOOK. Invent a compact outline icon of an open book or folded sheet using just an unmistakable capital M and an unmistakable C. The M supplies the central valley, page folds and upper page edges; the C supplies the curved outer page edge. A beautiful economical piece of visual wit. The finished icon should convincingly read both as a folded/open page and as MC. No literal extra book illustration, stacked books, boxes, circles or decorative lines. Avoid an M simply placed within a C. Think symbol construction rather than typed ligature.

The letter M becomes two spare angular folds of a sail, while a clearly open C curve becomes the lower sail and boat contour. Only two or three broad strokes, simple assured composition, no nautical badges, compass, waves, ropes or extra scenic detail. The object must feel like a quiet tiny sailing symbol, while capital M and C retain their recognizable letter anatomy. Clever integration with shared endpoints rather than separate MC text or an M inside a C circle. A refined personal emblem.

### 03-bird

A visual double reading: the actual strokes of the letters constitute the object. The client liked an example where a few rounded monogram strokes form a tulip, but DOES NOT WANT A FLOWER. No mockups, no shading, gradients, texture or 3D. Do not draw an object and then print MC inside or beside it. Keep the letter shapes recognizable. Concept: SMALL BIRD. Create an exceptionally simple outline bird symbol directly constructed out of an M and C. A crisp but gently rounded M makes two lifted wings; a single open C makes the body or breast and returns naturally into the wing construction. Very economical, poised, quiet and understated. Abstract bird rather than cartoon or detailed animal. No eyes, feathers, feet, branches, flowers or extra flourishes. Both capital initials remain legible through their intact strokes. Do not put MC inside a bird or enclose an M in a generic circle. The bird and letters should be one idea.
