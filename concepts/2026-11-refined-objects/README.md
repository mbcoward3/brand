## Exact prompts

### Initial book refinement

Use case: logo-brand refinement. Edit the supplied open-book MC concept. Preserve the simple open-book/folded-page theme, compact proportions, black monoline construction, white background and restraint. Make the initials unmistakable. The main correction is the M: replace the soft Y-shaped upper junction with an actual capital M made from a left upright, a clear diagonal descending to a deep central V, a diagonal ascending to the right shoulder, and a short right upright. Integrate these strokes as the page folds and upper outline of the open book. The curved page shape on the right must read as a C, open to the right, with a short clean gap and consistent stroke weight. Use the central book crease as a shared structural stroke where possible. The outer lower page edges should still suggest an open book. Remove any extra line that competes with the letters. Refine the optical balance and reduce the pill-shaped rounding: crisp deliberate internal joins, lightly softened outer corners and clean flat terminals. Aim for an elegant custom MC symbol with an open-book double meaning, rather than an ordinary book icon. Keep BOTH M and C legible, do not add text or supporting type. One logo centered on pure white with ample space. Flat uniform black, no textures, shadows, gray artifacts, gradients, decorative details or mockups.

### Initial two-peak refinement

Use case: logo-brand refinement. Edit the supplied angular M over a curved C concept. Preserve its distinctive two peaked sail/ear silhouette and open lower curve, so it retains the subtle small-boat / cat-ear double reading. Make the letters sharper and more intentional. Give the M a clear left stem, two precise peaks, a deep clean central V and a readable right stem; resolve the central diagonal so it does not turn into a third separate vertical sail mast or clutter the negative space. Give the lower C a balanced smooth curve and a visibly open right side, with upper and lower endpoints that make its C anatomy obvious. Integrate M and C through a shared stroke rather than stacking unrelated letters. Improve proportions, even stroke weight and negative space. Use pointed but tasteful internal joins, lightly softened exterior corners and clean flat-cut endpoints instead of bulbous rounded caps. Keep the same spare black monoline character and compact silhouette, no facial details, eyes, whiskers, waves, extra boat elements or literal illustration. The result should read MC first while the ear/sail form remains discoverable. Single logo centered on pure white, large margins, crisp flat black art, no other text, labels, gradients, shadows, textures or 3D.

### Clearer M within the book

Use case: logo-brand precise refinement. Edit this black outlined open book logo. The current upper strokes read Y, not M. Correct that. Keep the recognizable open-book silhouette and the C-shaped open right page. Reconstruct the LEFT PAGE as a complete clear capital M: its left upright is the left page edge, it has two distinct high shoulders and a deep interior diagonal V, and its right upright shares the book central spine. The entire M should fit on the left half of the open book, with all four classic M strokes visible. The RIGHT PAGE remains a capital C with its broad opening to the right. This produces MC side by side whose outer lower strokes and shared central spine form the open book. Make the upper left page have the necessary fold/zigzag for a real M instead of a single descending diagonal. Keep the rest of the icon spare, refine proportions as needed, medium weight black monoline, crisp joins and square terminals, slight optical softening only at exterior corners. The resulting MC should be legible immediately while retaining a book / folded-page interpretation. No extra text, no labels, no explanatory lettering, no illustrative details, no shadows, no texture. Single centered black logo on clean white.

### Mountains and lake refinement

Use case: logo-brand refinement. Edit the supplied MC symbol. The client sees MOUNTAINS AND A LAKE in it and likes that meaning. Develop that exact interpretation while keeping the recognizable initials M and C. Preserve the overall design: two simple M peaks above one broad open C curve that suggests a still lake. Refine, do not replace the idea. Improve the proportions: make the lake a little wider and slightly shallower, the mountain peaks balanced but not perfectly mirrored, and the stroke about 15 percent lighter for an understated refined feel. Crucially shorten/lift the central downward V tip of the M so it stops cleanly ABOVE the upper lake arc, with a deliberate small gap of white between the V and the lake line; avoid the pinched three-way junction of the reference. Keep M left and right outer stems joined naturally to the upper lake contour so the symbol remains unified. The lower C must retain a clear opening on the right with clean aligned flat-cut terminals, not become an O or U. Use two mountain peaks only. Keep M anatomy and C anatomy easy to trace. Sharpen the geometry with tasteful optical softening, smooth deliberate lake curves and evenly controlled weight. No extra waves, horizon lines, reflections, sun, trees, badges, framing circles or added landscape illustration. Single compact elegant MC logo centered on pure white, flat pure black strokes, no labels, typography, gradients, shadows or textures.
